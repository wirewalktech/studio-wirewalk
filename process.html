Studio / Process
Process

Nothing important is decided at the end.

The same shape whether it is a half-day of product stills, a three-day film or an audio mix — what changes is how long each stage takes. Everything that will be delivered (formats, counts, crops, rights and dates) is written down before the first frame is shot, because that is the argument nobody wants to have afterward.

Deliverables and formats Rights and usage
01 — The six stages

Brief to archive.

Two rounds of revision are inside the scope by default. Extra rounds are possible, and are agreed and priced before they start rather than absorbed silently and resented.

Brief and scope

A conversation about what this is for and where it will be seen, which is usually a different question from what was originally asked for. It ends in writing: the deliverable list, the counts, the aspect ratios, the usage rights, the dates, and what is explicitly not included. If the honest answer is that you do not need a shoot, that gets said here.

Recce and pre-production

For location work, a site visit or a detailed remote review: available light through the day, power, access, ceiling height, noise sources, and where a crew can stand without being in the way. For video, the script or the interview structure. For audio, a listen to the room. Call sheets, a shot list and a schedule that assumes something will run late.

Shoot or session

Tethered or monitored, so what is being captured is visible while there is still time to change it rather than discovered that evening. Anything that cannot be reshot — a live event, a one-time interview, a performance — is recorded redundantly. Cards and recorders are offloaded to two destinations before anything leaves the location.

Selects and first cut

An edited selection rather than a raw dump: the frames or the assembly worth spending time on, with the near-misses left out. For video, a rough cut with structure and timing settled before any grade or sound work, because that is what feedback should be given on. Sent as a private link with per-frame or per-timecode comments.

Revisions

The first round is structural: order, length, what is missing. The second is fine: word-level, frame-level, level-level. Splitting them this way stops a project cycling on details that a later structural change will throw away.

Delivery and archive

Final files in the agreed formats through a download link, or straight into your own storage if that is easier. Named to a convention you can search, with photographic metadata carrying caption, credit and usage. Masters and project files are kept for twelve months, so a re-version, a new crop or a translated cut does not mean starting again.

02 — Delivery

What lands, and in what format.

Defaults, not limits. If your platform, print house or broadcaster wants something else, say so in the brief and it is delivered that way — it is far cheaper to set a target before the session than to remaster afterward.

Photography

Masters
16-bit TIFF or layered PSD, retouching kept on separate layers
Web
sRGB JPEG at your template’s pixel dimensions, sharpened for screen
Print
Adobe RGB or ProPhoto, 300 ppi at final size, soft-proofed to a supplied profile
Crops
16:9, 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 versions framed at capture, not center-cropped afterward
Extras
Clipping paths, separated shadow layers, alternate angles
Metadata
IPTC caption, credit line, date and the agreed usage embedded in every file

Video

Masters
ProRes 422 HQ or 4444 up to UHD, graded, with and without titles
Web
H.264 or H.265 MP4 at platform-appropriate bitrate, fast-start
Ratios
16:9 master plus 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 cutdowns framed at capture
Text
SRT and VTT caption files, plus burned-in subtitle versions where required
Audio
Stereo mix embedded, with dialogue, music and effects stems alongside
Project
Timeline and graded master retained for twelve months for re-versioning

Audio

Capture
24-bit WAV at 48 kHz for picture, 96 kHz where the source justifies it
Mix
Stereo, checked for mono compatibility; isolated channels retained
Loudness
−23 LUFS broadcast, −16 LUFS spoken-word platforms, −14 LUFS music streaming — measured, not estimated
Peak
True-peak headroom left to survive lossy encoding
Stems
Dialogue, music and effects delivered separately for re-versioning and translation
Archive
Session files and raw takes held for twelve months
03 — Rights

Licensing is written down before the shoot, not negotiated after it.

Business buyers care about this more than about any other part of a proposal, and most photographers’ sites are vague about it. Here it is in plain terms.

  • Usage is defined in the scopeWhere the work may be used, in which territories, for how long, and whether that includes paid media. The license is written into the same document as the deliverable list, so there is one place to look.
  • Broad and perpetual licences are availableAnd are priced as such. There is no attempt to make a wide license difficult in order to sell it again later.
  • Full copyright transfer is possibleWhere a project genuinely requires it — some regulated, defense and confidential work does — it is agreed explicitly and in writing rather than assumed by either side.
  • The license travels with the fileAgreed usage is embedded in each image’s IPTC metadata, so when a picture is forwarded internally two years later, the terms go with it instead of being lost with the email.
  • Raw files and unmixed sessions stay with the studio by defaultThey are the working material rather than the deliverable, and releasing them without the grade, the retouch or the mix produces work that misrepresents both sides. Where a project needs them — an in-house team taking over post, for instance — that is a scope item, not a refusal.
  • Confidential work is not shown anywhereIncluding in this site’s own work section, without written permission. Work made under a mutual non-disclosure agreement stays unpublished by default, which is why the gallery is largely personal work rather than commissions.
  • People photographed in publicDocumentary and street frames in the gallery are of members of the public and carry no model release. They are shown as portfolio work only. They are not used to imply that anybody pictured endorses the studio, and they are not licensed on for commercial or advertising use.
04 — Booking terms

Rates, retainers and payment.

The standard terms below apply to Studio bookings. Your written quote records the scope, dates, deliverables and usage rights before you book. Current service fees come from the booking system.

Payment

A 25% retainer

The retainer is 25% of the booking fee, rounded to the nearest whole dollar. It holds your agreed date and is credited against the total; the balance is due on delivery. Newborn sessions hold a provisional week, not a particular day, and the session is confirmed once the baby arrives. Confirm your date or provisional week with us before paying.

Cancellation

14 days or more: a full retainer refund

If you cancel 14 or more days before the session, the retainer is refunded in full. With less than 14 days’ notice, the retainer is non-refundable, but you may move it once to a new date within six months.

Family and newborn

Illness and rain: a free reschedule

If anyone is unwell or rain means a family or newborn session needs to move, it is rescheduled at no charge, whenever you tell us. This exception overrides the standard cancellation and one-transfer limits above.

If we cancel

Your choice: a refund or a new date

If the studio cancels, you choose a full refund of everything you have paid or a new date.

Delivery

Dates you can plan around

Personal and family galleries arrive within two weeks; business photography within five business days. Events include same-night selects, with the full edit within two weeks.

Video and audio are delivered within three weeks of the final brief and assets, plus revision rounds. Two rounds of revision are included; further rounds are quoted. Rush delivery is agreed in the written quote.

In full or invoiced

5% off when paid in full

Full payment up front takes 5% off; this discount does not apply to a retainer. Commercial work may instead be invoiced against a purchase order on the agreed invoice terms.

Tax and travel

Agreed before you book

Prices exclude applicable sales tax, which is calculated and invoiced separately. Travel beyond the metro area is quoted before you book.

Rights and replies

In writing, within one business day

We follow up on requests within one business day. Usage rights are stated in the written quote; personal print rights and commercial licensing are explained in rights and usage.
