Family and children

The year they were like this.

Children change faster than anyone photographs them. A family session is not really about a portrait for the wall — though you get one — it is about having a proper record of a particular summer, a particular height, a particular way of standing. Sessions are run at the pace of the youngest person there, which is the only pace that produces anything worth keeping.

01 — Sessions

Five kinds of family work.

All of them run the same way: unhurried, mostly outdoors or in your own house, with as little posing as the picture will tolerate. Children who are being arranged look like children who are being arranged.

Together

Family sessions

The whole group, in a park, on a beach, in the garden or at home. Usually about ninety minutes, which sounds long and is not: the first twenty are spent letting everybody stop being self-conscious, and the pictures start after that. A handful of arranged group frames — the ones grandparents want — are done early while attention lasts, and the rest of the session is left to happen.

Children

Children and milestone portraits

First birthdays, starting school, a last summer before something changes. Shot at the child’s eye level rather than from adult height, which is the single biggest difference between a photograph of a child and a photograph taken over one. Milestone sessions are shorter, typically forty-five minutes to an hour, because that is roughly how long a small person stays interested.

Groups

Siblings and extended family

Brothers and sisters together, cousins, three or four generations in one frame when everybody is in the same place for once. Large groups are photographed early in the session and in open shade, because the alternative is twenty-two people squinting into low sun while somebody blinks. Individual portraits of each child are made in the same session and delivered alongside.

At home

At-home lifestyle sessions

Breakfast, the garden, the sofa everybody actually sits on, the dog that will not be excluded. Available light, no equipment cluttering the room, and no request that anybody tidy up first — the mess is half of what the photographs are for. Especially good for babies, toddlers and anyone who behaves differently the moment they leave the house.

Yearly

Seasonal and annual updates

The same family, the same time each year, often the same place. The value compounds: after four or five years you have a sequence rather than a set of unrelated pictures. Framing, distance and treatment are kept consistent from year to year so the sequence reads as one thing, and the notes from each session are retained to make that possible.

02 — Newborn

Booked around a due date, not a date.

Newborn sessions are the one thing on this site that cannot be scheduled normally, because babies do not arrive on the day they are meant to. A provisional week is held rather than a day, and the session is confirmed once the baby is actually here.

  • The first two weeks are the windowNewborns sleep deeply and curl naturally for roughly the first fortnight. After about three weeks they are more awake, more startled and much harder to settle, which changes the character of the pictures. It is not too late at four weeks; it is simply a different session.
  • Long and slowTwo to three hours, most of which is not photography. Feeding, settling, changing, feeding again. A session booked at ninety minutes becomes a stressful session, and stressed newborns photograph exactly as stressed.
  • A warm, quiet roomWarmer than adults find comfortable — a newborn who is cold will not settle. Your home is usually the best place: everything the baby needs is already there and nobody has to travel with a five-day-old.
  • Parents and siblings in frameThe photographs people keep longest are rarely the baby alone. Hands, the crook of an arm, an older sibling being extremely careful. Siblings are photographed early, before a toddler’s patience runs out.
  • Posed or lifestyle, and the difference mattersLifestyle means the baby as they are: in a parent’s arms, on the bed, in natural light. Posed means supported, settled positions built up slowly. Posed work is done conservatively and with a parent’s hands always within reach — no unsupported positions, no composites that require a baby to be balanced. Safety decides the pose, not the other way round.
  • If anyone is unwell, it movesSame as any session here. No charge, no argument, no attempt to talk you into it.

03 — What you get

The practical part.

Typical figures, confirmed in writing when the session is booked so there is nothing to interpret afterwards. Where a family wants more or less than the usual, it is agreed then rather than negotiated at delivery.

The session

Family
About 90 minutes, at home or on location
Children
45–60 minutes, run at the child’s pace
Newborn
2–3 hours, most of it feeding and settling
Where
Your home, a park, a beach — somewhere the children already know, where possible
When
Scheduled for the light rather than the diary: the hour after sunrise or the two before sunset

The files

Selection
A gallery of the frames worth keeping, typically 40–60 from a family session
Editing
Every delivered image individually colour-corrected and finished, not batch-processed
Turnaround
A short preview within a few days; the finished gallery usually inside two to three weeks, and the date is fixed when you book
Sizes
Full-resolution files for print and web-sized versions for sharing, both included
Rights
Yours to print and share personally, in writing, with no print package to buy first

Prints and albums

Prints
Through a professional lab, colour-managed and soft-proofed, in the sizes you choose
Framing
Mounted and framed prints arranged where wanted; you are equally welcome to print elsewhere
Albums
Layflat albums designed from the session, laid out and sent for approval before anything is made
Optional
None of this is required. The files are the deliverable; prints and albums are a separate choice.
Archive
Your gallery is kept for twelve months so a reprint does not mean a rescue operation
04 — What to expect

With small children, the plan is to have very little plan.

Most of what makes a family session work is decided before anyone picks up a camera, and almost none of it is photographic.

Timing

Go around the nap, never through it

Tell me when they nap and when they eat, and the session is built around it. A tired two-year-old is not a photography problem that can be solved with patience; it is a tired two-year-old.

Behaviour

Nobody has to smile

Children are not asked to look at the camera or say anything. They are given something to do — a hill to run down, water to stand in, a job — and photographed doing it. The frames that end up on the wall are almost always the ones where nobody was performing.

Clothes

Comfortable beats coordinated

Colours that sit together rather than match exactly, and nothing anybody is worried about getting muddy, because they will. A child who has been told to keep their clothes clean stops moving, and a child who has stopped moving is not worth photographing.

Shyness

The first twenty minutes are free

Some children hide, and that is normal and accounted for in the session length. No child is bribed, hurried or made to do anything, and if one wants nothing to do with it for half an hour, that half hour is theirs.

Illness

If a child is unwell, we move it

Say so and the session is rescheduled at no cost, however late the notice. This matters more than anything else on this page: a family that feels obliged to bring a sick child to a booking they have paid for gets bad photographs and a bad memory attached to them. It is a session, not an appointment you can miss.

Weather

Rain moves it too

Overcast is excellent and needs no change. Actual rain gets rescheduled, unless the family fancies it, in which case wellingtons and puddles make some of the best pictures anyone gets.
