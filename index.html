01 — What the studio does

Three disciplines that keep failing at the joins.

Most production problems are handover problems. The photographer delivers files nobody can use at the size they need; the editor gets rushes in the wrong colour space; the video is finished with whatever audio the camera happened to record. Doing all three in one place removes the joins, which is the whole argument for working this way.

03 — Business and corporate

The pictures a company keeps needing.

Executive and team headshots that match across a whole leadership page, including the joiner photographed eight months later. People actually at work, which is what most companies mean when they ask for headshots. Workplace, floor space and facility. Culture imagery for careers pages. Conferences and award evenings. And, done properly, one planned shoot producing a reusable library for the site, the deck, social and the press office, instead of commissioning the same image four times at four times the cost.

Two things separate a professional engagement from a good camera: usage rights stated up front, so legal and marketing both know what they have; and delivery in the exact crops and formats your channels need, rather than a folder of full-frame files somebody else has to cut up.

04 — How it runs

Nothing is decided at the end.

Formats, counts, crops, usage rights and dates are written down before the first frame is shot. It is the single thing that keeps a production from turning into an argument about what was implied, and it costs one conversation.

Brief and scope

What it is for and where it will be seen — usually a different question from what was first asked for. Ends in writing: deliverables, counts, ratios, usage, dates, and what is explicitly not included.

Recce and pre-production

Light through the day, power, access, noise, and where a crew can stand without being in the way. Scripts and interview structure for video. A listen to the room for audio.

Shoot or session

Tethered or monitored, so what is being captured is visible while there is still time to change it. Anything unrepeatable is recorded twice. Cards offloaded to two destinations before anyone leaves.

Selects, edit and delivery

An edited selection rather than a raw dump, two rounds of revision built in, then final files named to a convention you can search, with metadata and rights embedded.
