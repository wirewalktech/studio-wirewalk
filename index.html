01

Family and children

Children change faster than anyone photographs them. A family session is a proper record of a particular summer, a particular height, a particular way of standing — not just a portrait for the wall, though you get one of those too.

Sessions run at the pace of the youngest person there, which is the only pace that produces anything worth keeping. Mostly outdoors or in your own house, with as little posing as the picture will tolerate. A handful of arranged group frames — the ones grandparents want — are made early while attention lasts; the rest is left to happen.

Newborn sessions are booked around a due date rather than a day, held as a provisional week and confirmed once the baby arrives. The first fortnight is the window. And if a child is unwell, the session moves at no charge, however late the notice.

Pets

Almost entirely about time and location. An animal asked to sit still in an unfamiliar studio gives you one kind of picture; the same animal on a beach at the hour it likes best gives you another, and the second kind is what people keep.

Beach, woodland, snow and open ground at first or last light, or available light at home for older and nervous animals. Fast shutter, continuous autofocus, and enough distance that the animal forgets there is a camera. Owners in frame or out, as you prefer.

Sessions are booked with slack in them on purpose. Nothing useful comes out of the first ten minutes with a dog that has just arrived somewhere new, and the good frames arrive once everybody has stopped trying.

Business and commercial

The pictures a company keeps needing: headshots that match across a whole leadership page, people actually at work, the workplace itself, culture imagery for careers pages, and one planned shoot that produces a reusable library instead of four urgent commissions.

Team headshots are shot to one agreed look and the setup is recorded, so a joiner photographed eight months later matches the people they stand beside on the page. Sittings run on a short slot each, on a schedule that fits a working day, with a compact lighting setup that fits a meeting room rather than needing one cleared.

Usage rights are stated up front — where the images may be used, in which territories, for how long, and whether paid media is included — and delivery is in the exact crops and formats your channels take, not a folder of full-frame files somebody has to cut up. Screens, whiteboards and documents are watched for and composed out; work under NDA where required.

07

Real estate and interiors

Two failures account for most bad property photography: converging verticals left to a slider afterwards, and interiors exposed either for the room or for the window but never both. Both are fixed at capture.

Shift movements keep verticals vertical; blended exposures keep a window showing its view while the room stays the colour it actually is. Rooms shot wide but not distorted, at the height a person sees from rather than at chest height on a tripod. Hero exteriors scheduled for the hour the building faces the light, which for most properties is a single window in the day.

Residential listings and developments, commercial floor space for lettings and tenders, and the close detail that sells a specification — joinery, stone, ironmongery, the way a handle meets a door.

Audio

Nobody leaves a video because the grade was a shade cool. They leave because the room is ringing, the level jumps between speakers, or the music is sitting on top of the dialogue.

Audio is treated as its own discipline with its own session, not as something applied to a video timeline at the end of a long day. Location and controlled-room recording, voice and dialogue, podcast production, mixing, mastering, and full post against a locked picture.

Rooms are listened to before anything is plugged in: a hard-surfaced meeting room with a glass wall is a worse problem than any microphone can solve, and the fix is moving, treating or rescheduling rather than repairing it afterwards.

How a booking runs

The same shape whether it is a family session, a day of headshots or a three-day film. What is being delivered — counts, crops, usage rights and dates — is written down before the first frame is shot.

Brief and quote

What it is for and where it will be seen. Ends in writing: deliverables, counts, ratios, usage, dates, the price, and what is explicitly not included. Nothing is booked before you have that.

Planning

Light through the day, access and logistics. For family work: naps, meals and where the children are already comfortable. For video, the script. For audio, a listen to the room.

The session

Tethered or monitored, so what is being captured is visible while there is still time to change it. Anything unrepeatable is recorded twice, and cards are offloaded to two destinations before anyone leaves.

Edit and delivery

An edited selection rather than a raw dump, two rounds of revision built in, then final files named to a convention you can search, with metadata and rights embedded. Masters kept for twelve months.
