Photography

Every kind of photography the studio takes on.

A photograph is rarely used the way it was framed. It gets squared off for a grid, cut to a banner, dropped onto a printed page nobody colour-checked, or handed to a platform that centre-crops without asking. Everything below is shot with that afterlife in mind: room in the frame, exposure that holds up under compression, and a colour path that does not fall apart between the camera and whoever ingests the file.

01 — People and portraits

Faces, and the places they belong to.

Studio sittings on a controlled setup, environmental portraits where somebody actually spends their day, and street portraiture made with permission and a short conversation rather than a long lens. Retouching stays at the level where the person still recognises themselves.

Studio

Sittings and headshots

One light and a reflector more often than four heads. The point of a portrait sitting is the twenty minutes of conversation that make somebody stop performing, and equipment that takes ten minutes to move works against that. Team and executive work — where matching a whole set matters more than any single frame — is covered under business photography.

On location

Environmental portraits

A person photographed in the place that explains them: the workshop, the counter, the boat, the office floor. Lit to sit inside the ambient rather than on top of it, because a portrait that obviously had a flash fired at it stops being about the place.

Street

Documentary portraiture

The frames below are from the studio’s own street work. They are made close and made with consent: asking costs a picture perhaps a third of the time and gets a better one the rest of it. This is also the discipline that trains everything else — reading light quickly, framing once, and knowing when the moment has already gone.

02 — Events

Coverage that does not become part of the event.

Conferences, panels, launches, performance and award evenings. Quiet, low-profile, and fast where it needs to be: a tight edited selection goes out the same night or the next morning, because press and social need pictures while the thing is still happening rather than after the full edit lands.

Corporate

Conferences and panels

Keynote, breakouts, exhibition floor, and the reception afterwards, where the useful pictures usually are. Speaker names and titles checked against the programme so captions do not have to be reverse-engineered later. Scoped together with headshots and workplace work under business photography.

Stage

Performance and awards

Working in whatever the lighting designer decided, without a flash that would ruin it for everyone in the room. Fast primes, high ISO handled properly at capture rather than rescued afterwards, and a position agreed with the production team before doors.

Turnaround

Same-night selects

An edited set, captioned and correctly sized for the channels using it, delivered while the event is running or first thing the following morning. The full take follows once it has been properly worked.

03 — Weddings

The day, not the shot list.

A shot list exists and gets delivered, because the family group nobody photographed is the one thing that cannot be fixed afterwards. But the pictures anybody looks at in ten years are the ones between the items on it.

Before

Planning the run of the day

A timeline built backwards from the light: where the sun is at the ceremony, where it is at the portraits, and how long the walk between them takes. Most wedding photography problems are scheduling problems dressed as photography problems.

During

Ceremony and coverage

Two bodies, quiet shutters, no direction during the ceremony itself. Group photographs done quickly and by name from a list agreed in advance, so the part everyone dreads takes fifteen minutes rather than an hour.

After

Delivery

An edited set rather than every frame, in web and print sizes, with a small preview selection within a few days and the full edit to the agreed date. Files are yours to print; no print-package requirement.

Tethered and stacked

Shot tethered to a calibrated display, so what is being captured is judged full size while the set is still standing. Focus-stacked where the depth of the object demands it, which is most jewellery, most tools and anything photographed close.

05 — Real estate and interiors

Verticals vertical, windows still showing something.

Two failures account for most bad property photography: converging verticals left to a slider afterwards, and interiors exposed either for the room or for the window but never both. Both are fixed at capture, with shift movements and blended exposures, and neither costs extra time once it is how you work.

Residential

Listings and developments

Room sets shot wide but not distorted, at the height a person sees from rather than at chest height on a tripod. Hero exteriors scheduled for the hour the building faces the light, which for most properties is a single window in the day.

Commercial

Workplace and facility

Office floors, retail, hospitality and industrial space, for lettings, tenders and capability material. Frequently commissioned alongside workplace photography, which is the same visit with people in it.

Detail

Materials and finish

The close work that sells a specification: joinery, stone, ironmongery, the way a handle meets a door. Usually the difference between a listing that looks like every other listing and one that does not.

06 — Pets

At the animal’s pace, not the diary’s.

Pet photography is almost entirely about time and location. An animal that is being asked to sit still in an unfamiliar studio produces one kind of picture; the same animal on a beach or a snow track at the hour it likes best produces another. The second kind is what people keep.

Outdoors

On the move

Beach, woodland, snow and open ground, at first or last light. Fast shutter, continuous autofocus, and enough distance that the animal forgets there is a camera. Owners in frame or out, as you prefer.

At home

Where they are comfortable

Available light in the house, on the furniture they are not supposed to be on. Older and nervous animals are almost always better photographed at home than anywhere else.

Time

Sessions run long on purpose

Booked with slack in them. Nothing useful comes from the first ten minutes with a dog that has just arrived somewhere new, and the good frames tend to arrive once everybody has stopped trying.

The dish, the room, and the people running it.

Dishes at pass speed

Ground

Landscape and coast

Large-format discipline on small cameras: tripod, considered position, one frame rather than forty. Long exposures where moving water or cloud is doing the work, and blended exposures where the range genuinely exceeds the sensor rather than because it is easier.

Air

Drone work

Elevated and overhead frames for property, development, infrastructure and coastline. Flown legally: airspace checked, permissions obtained where the site requires them, and flights planned around people on the ground rather than around the shot. Where a site cannot be flown, a mast or a high vantage point usually gets most of the way there.

Print

Sized for the wall

Landscape work is delivered soft-proofed for print at the size it is going to be made, in the profile the lab or printer supplies. A file optimised for a screen and a file optimised for a two-metre print are not the same file.
