Studio / Business photography
Business and corporate

Photography a company can actually use.

Most corporate photography fails for administrative reasons rather than aesthetic ones. The headshots do not match because they were taken by five people over five years. The files arrive full-frame and somebody in marketing spends a week cropping them. Nobody can answer whether the images may be used in a paid campaign, so they are quietly used anyway. This page is about the parts that prevent that.

01 — Scope

What businesses actually commission.

Seven kinds of work, usually bought separately and at a premium, that sit far more cheaply inside a single planned engagement. Sections that overlap with the wider practice link across rather than repeating themselves.

Consistency

Executive and team headshots

Shot to one agreed look — the same background, the same key and fill, the same crop and the same retouching standard — so a leadership page or a staff directory reads as one set rather than an assembly of whatever each person had to hand. Lighting positions, lens, distance and background are recorded at the first session, which means a joiner photographed eight months later matches the people they are standing beside on the page. Matching new starters to an existing set is part of the service, not a new shoot.

People

Environmental portraits at work

What most companies actually want when they ask for headshots: the engineer at the bench, the surgeon in theatre, the trader at the desk, lit properly and shot where the work happens. These carry a story a plain backdrop cannot, they date far more slowly, and they crop well into the wide banners and narrow columns that websites and reports keep needing.

Place

Workplace, office and facility

Interiors, floor space, plant and equipment, operations in progress. Useful for careers pages, capability decks, tenders, investor material and property marketing. Shot with the perspective control an interior needs, exposure-blended so windows keep their view, and scheduled around the hours when the space looks like itself rather than the hours that were convenient to book.

Recruiting

Team and culture imagery

Real working moments for careers and recruiting pages: collaboration that is actually happening, the space people occupy, the parts of the day a candidate would recognise. Directed lightly rather than staged, because staged culture photography is transparent and does more harm to a careers page than no photography at all.

Live

Corporate events and conferences

Keynotes, panels, launches, exhibition floor and award evenings, with speaker names and titles checked against the programme and a same-night selection for press and social while the event is still running. The full approach is under event photography — for a corporate programme it is usually scoped together with the headshots and the workplace work, in one visit.

Commercial

Product and packaging

Product, packaging and pack-shot work for commercial use, tethered and consistent across a range. The craft detail is under product photography. What matters here is that commercial licensing, including paid media, is priced and written into the same agreement as the rest of the engagement.

External

PR, press and reporting

Press portraits supplied at wire-service resolution with correct IPTC caption, credit and usage embedded, so a picture desk can use them without calling. Imagery for annual reports and investor material, shot to the page grid where one exists and delivered with the bleed and colour profile the printer specifies.

02 — The efficient way to buy it

One planned shoot beats six urgent ones.

The expensive pattern is commissioning reactively: a headshot for a press release, a floor picture for a tender, three product shots for a deck, each booked at short notice with its own minimum. A library shoot covers the year’s foreseeable needs in one or two days.

  • Scoped from your channels backwardsThe list starts from where images actually get used — the website templates, the deck master, the social formats, the careers page, the press kit — and works back to a shot list, rather than starting from a shot list and hoping it fits.
  • Shot for the crops you will needFramed at capture with room for 16:9, 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16, and for the awkward wide banner that every site has. Cropping to a ratio you did not plan for is how a good photograph becomes an unusable one.
  • Delivered as a catalogue, not a folderNamed to a convention you can search, tagged by subject and by intended use, with caption, credit and the agreed usage embedded in each file so the licence travels with the image when it is forwarded internally.
  • ExtensibleThe lighting and framing notes are kept, so an addition next quarter matches rather than restarting the look.

03 — The engagement

What separates a professional engagement from a good camera.

None of the following is about equipment. All of it is about whether the pictures are usable, defensible and repeatable a year from now.

Licensing

Usage rights stated up front

The agreement says where the images may be used, in which territories, for how long, and whether paid media is included. Broad, worldwide and perpetual licences are all available and are priced as such; full transfer of copyright is possible where a project genuinely requires it and is agreed explicitly rather than assumed. Most photographers’ sites are vague here, which is exactly why it becomes a problem two years later when a picture turns up in an advertising campaign nobody licensed it for. Full terms are set out under rights and usage.

Delivery

The formats and crops your channels need

Web files at the pixel dimensions your templates take, sharpened for screen. Print files at 300 ppi at final size in the profile your printer asks for, soft-proofed. Social crops framed at capture. Directory and CMS images at the exact aspect ratio the platform enforces, so nothing gets centre-cropped through somebody’s forehead. The list is agreed in the brief; see deliverables and formats.

Standard

Retouching to one agreed level

The retouching standard is set before the first sitting and applied identically across the set: blemishes and stray hair, yes; reshaped faces, no, unless you ask and everyone photographed knows. Consistency matters more than degree — a page where one person has been smoothed and the rest have not is more conspicuous than a page where nobody has.

On site

Logistics handled around a working office

A schedule that fits the working day, with short sitting times per person for team shoots — typically a few minutes each on a running slot, so nobody loses a morning — and a running order that can absorb the two people who will inevitably be late. A compact lighting setup that fits a meeting room rather than needing one cleared. Where a brand guideline exists, it is read first and worked to: background colour, crop, aspect ratio, tone.

Confidentiality

Discretion on site

Screens, whiteboards, documents, prototypes andnameplates all end up in frame if nobody is watching for them. They are watched for, and anything sensitive is composed out or cleared before the frame is taken rather than retouched afterwards. Work is done under a mutual non-disclosure agreement where required, and images made under one are not shown anywhere, including in this site’s own work section, without written permission.
